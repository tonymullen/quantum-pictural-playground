A companion to Picturing Quantum Processes

Learning Quantum Picturalism with the Quantum Pictural Playground

A tutorial course in drawing, computing and proving with quantum processes, chapter by chapter alongside Coecke and Kissinger’s Picturing Quantum Processes .

What this book is

Picturing Quantum Processes (from here on PQP) teaches quantum theory through diagrams: processes are boxes, systems are wires, and you calculate by redrawing pictures. This book doesn’t replace PQP. It is a workbook to use beside it. Each chapter follows the PQP chapter with the same number, starting at Chapter 3, and has you build PQP’s key diagrams in the Quantum Pictural Playground, check its equations, and solve exercises of increasing difficulty.

The book has two aims:

  • To make you fluent in the Playground. Each feature is introduced in the chapter where PQP first needs it, then used again in later chapters.
  • To give you quantum picturalism as a working tool. You should come away able to use diagrams to understand quantum information and to solve problems in it, not just to redraw known results.

Where it helps, the book also connects the pictures to the notation of most quantum-information courses: Dirac notation and, later, density matrices. Those connections are marked Two notations, are made term by term, and cite a standard source for the familiar side.

How to use it

  • Keep PQP open. The book cites PQP by section, equation and result number (for example §3.4.4, (3.16), Theorem 3.22), using the authors’ freely available PDF. The arguments are PQP’s; here you build them.
  • Keep the Playground open in another tab. Links marked ↗ open it on a particular workbook. A link you open from the book never overwrites your saved work.
  • Do the exercises in the Playground. Each exercise has a starter workbook, found under ▤ Workbooks ▸ Demos ▸ Textbook in a folder for its chapter. Its Exercise panel shows the task and, for proofs, your progress.
  • Use the hints sparingly. Each exercise has a few hints in the book, from a nudge to an outline. There are no worked solutions. The Playground checks your work as you go, so you know whether you have finished.

Exercise levels: ★ Warm-up, building or reproducing something with guidance; ★★ Practice, applying an idea to a new case; ★★★ Challenge, a proof, an explanation or a small investigation.

Before you begin

The Playground runs in your browser. Open it with the button in the sidebar, or with 📖 Textbook from the Playground back to here. A few things to know first:

  • What you save is a workbook: its circuits, diagrams and proofs, and the panels that show them. ▤ Workbooks opens the Workbook Browser.
  • Demos, including every textbook demo and exercise starter, are read-only. Change them as much as you like, then use Save a copy… to keep your version in My Workbooks.
  • The window is divided into panels, each showing one editor. Choose the editor in the panel’s header menu. Split or merge panels by dragging a panel’s corner grip, and resize them by dragging the borders between them.

One process theory

PQP develops diagrams for any process theory (PQP §3.1.2), and uses several: functions, relations, linear maps, quantum processes. The Playground computes in one of them: every wire is a qubit, and a diagram is a linear map, or a quantum process once diagrams are doubled (PQP Chapter 6). When a chapter’s point holds in every process theory, the book says so, and says where the Playground can only illustrate it.
